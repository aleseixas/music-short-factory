1. U.S. Copyright Office / Library of Congress — “Celebrating Women Through Their Copyright Story: Dolly Parton and Whitney Houston”
https://blogs.loc.gov/copyright/2023/03/celebrating-women-through-their-copyright-story-dolly-parton-and-whitney-houston/
Used for: Dolly Parton owning the composition and publishing rights; Elvis Presley wanting to record the song; Colonel Tom Parker requesting half of the publishing rights; Dolly declining that condition.

2. Library of Congress / National Recording Preservation Board — “I Will Always Love You” essay by Gerrick Kennedy
https://www.loc.gov/static/programs/national-recording-preservation-board/documents/I-Will-Always-Love-You_Kennedy.pdf
Used for: the song being written around Dolly Parton’s split from Porter Wagoner; reaching No. 1 on the country chart; Elvis’s interest; Kevin Costner suggesting the song for The Bodyguard; Costner advocating the a cappella opening.

3. Library of Congress / National Recording Registry — Women on the Recording Registry
https://www.loc.gov/programs/national-recording-preservation-board/recording-registry/descriptions-and-essays/women-on-the-recording-registry/
Used for: Whitney Houston’s 1992 recording becoming her signature song, topping charts, and its inclusion in the National Recording Registry.

4. Recording Academy / GRAMMY — “GRAMMY Rewind: Whitney Houston Admires Dolly Parton After ‘I Will Always Love You’ Wins In 1994”
https://www.grammy.com/news/whitney-houston-i-will-always-love-you-grammys-win-1994-dolly-parton-video-rewind/
Used for: Whitney winning Best Female Pop Vocal Performance; the recording also winning Record Of The Year; Dolly Parton presenting the award to Whitney.
