FACT SOURCES

1. El País — “El plagio de la lambada” (3 Oct 1989)
https://elpais.com/diario/1989/10/03/cultura/623372405_850215.html
Used for: Los Kjarkas identifying Kaoma’s Lambada as a copy of Llorando se fue; Ulises and Gonzalo Hermosa as the Bolivian composers; the commitment to recognize their rights and correct authorship.

2. El País — “Kjarkas: ‘La lambada es un dolor’” (29 May 1990)
https://elpais.com/diario/1990/05/29/cultura/643932017_850215.html
Used for: Llorando se fue being recorded by Los Kjarkas in 1981; judges ruling that the song popularized by Kaoma copied that composition; the copyright dispute that followed.

3. Le Monde — “Lambada : une carrière internationale aux origines troubles” (20 Sep 1989)
https://www.lemonde.fr/archives/article/1989/09/20/lambada-une-carriere-internationale-aux-origines-troubles_4131925_1819218.html
Used for: the song’s route from Bolivia to Brazil and France; Márcia Ferreira’s Brazilian version; Olivier Lorsac registering the song under the pseudonym Chico de Oliveira; Lambada’s major European success.

4. BBC News Brasil, republished by UOL — “Loalwa Braz e Kaoma conquistaram o mundo com plágio de canção boliviana” (20 Jan 2017)
https://musica.uol.com.br/noticias/bbc/2017/01/20/loalwa-braz-e-kaoma-conquistaram-o-mundo-com-plagio-de-cancao-boliviana.amp.htm
Used for: Márcia Ferreira’s 1986 Portuguese recording; Olivier Lorsac hearing the song in Porto Seguro; formation of Kaoma and selection of Brazilian singer Loalwa Braz; the 1991 legal action recognizing rights connected to the earlier versions.
